* Maxwell v Dinis // https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/10/court-to-consider-federal-inmates-ability-to-use-habeas-to-accelerate-transfer-to-a-halfway-hous/
 - In 2007 Maxwell was part of an organized crime scheme to take over and loot a Texas mortgage company. He was the crime family's lawyer and received illegitimate consulting payments. In 2011 he was convicted of racketeering, various frauds, and firearms crimes. He was sentenced to 20 years in prison. Maxwell participated in First Step act programs which entitle him to time credit to reduce his sentence. The BoP accelerates placement to Residential Reentry Centers (RRC, halfway house) or home confinement based on these time credits. Maxwell argues that the BoP miscalculated his time credits and accelerated placement. Maxwell made a series of administrative appeals for accelerated transfer to home confinement. The appeals were denied. Maxwell then brought a habeas petition seeking priority transfer, claims for FSA time credits, preferential housing, additional phone minutes, expanded commissary, and transfer to RRC. The district court (E.D.Tx.) dismissed finding that the BoP has discretion in granting request for transfer to home confinement, and finding that the Maxwell failed to exhast adminitrative remedies for the rest of his claims. Maxwell appealed the failure to exhaust for the FSA time credits. The 5th circuit affirmed the dismissal, finding that Habeas isn't the proper vehicle for challenges to conditions of confinement. Now Maxwell appeals to SCOTUS.
** Can an FSA claim seeking accelerated transfer to RRC or home confinement be brought under Habeas?
- Scalia wrote that habeas relief "includes ordering a quantum change in the level of custody". Ginsburg later quoted that in a majority (in tortured prose).
- 2255(e) requires habeas petitions to "test the legality of (petitioner's) detention". Maxwell's detention is in a prison and he claims the BoP violates the law by denying him transfer to home confinement.
- Maxwell cannot bring this case under 1983 as it is inapplicable against federal agents. And he cannot bring it under APA because it's explicitly inapplicable here (18 usc 3625)
- The government argues that habeas cannot chllange "level of custody."
- The government argues that statute doesn't allow courts to review BoP place of imprisonment decisions. "[A] designation of a place of imprisonment under this subsection is not reviewable by any court."
- An amicus was appointed to defend the 5th circuit's decision. The amicus argues that the 5th circuit's bright line rule is good. Bright line rules are good and line drawing is bad. "If a prisoner’s claim implies (1) that his physical detention must be shortened or terminated, (2) that he is entitled to earlier release on bail or parole, or (3) that he is being held in a place that the sentencing court did not have jurisdiction to send him, the prisoner’s claim sounds in habeas; otherwise, it does not." * Younge v Fulton County District Attorney's Office, Gerogia // https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/younge-v-fulton-judicial-circuit-district-attorneys-office/
- Younge worked for the DA's office until she was fired in July 2019. She had recently shared that she was pregnant. Younge sued based on discrimination. After discovery the DA's office moved for summary judgement arguing that Younge fell under a "personal-staff exception" to such claims. Younge argues that the DA can't use that argument because they didn't raise it in their response to her complaint. Affirmative defenses must be raised in response to a complaint, the personal-staff exception wasn't raised, so it was waived. The district court ruled that the defense could be raised and ruled on the summary judgement in favor of the DA. The 11th circuit affirmed, finding that there was no prejudice to allowing the late-raised defense.

* St. Mary v Roy
- Colorado's universal preschool program pays for children's preschooling at approved preschools. Approved preschools must be liscenced, deliver atleast 15 instruction hours per week, comply with disability and IEP requirements, train and liscence special education teachers, offer free tution for eligible families, maintain liability insurance, and comply with the non-discrimination mandate. The nondiscrimination mandate disallows preschools from denying enrollment based on race, ethnicity, religion, lack of housing, income, disabilitiy, sexual orientation, or gender identity. St. Mary requested an excemption allowing them to deny LGBTQ children and children of LGBTQ parents. Colorado denied the excemption. St. Mary, along with the archdiocese and two parent's of preschool aged children sued, arguing that the nondiscrimination requirement violates the first amendment. The district court dismissed the case and the 10th circuit affirmed, finding that the program did not unduly burden religious practice. The nondiscrimination requirement was neutral and not generally applicable. Now St. Mary appeals to SCOTUS.

*** Carson v Makin
- Maine create a tuition assistance program. The schools must be nonsectarian and cannot promote a faith or belief system. The court found this violated free exercise under strict scrutiny.
** What does St. Mary have to show to prove lack of "general applicability" under Employment Dvision v Smith?
- Colorado's professed interest is equal opportunity to enroll and recieve preschool regardless of protected characteristics. Colorado allows some UPK schools to deny families based on income level. Colorado schools allows some UPK schools to enroll only children with a disability. Odean, director of the UPK program, also testified that UPK schools could admit only gender-nonconforming children, children of color, or lgbtq children. Colorado can't deny UPK access to St. Mary because of discrimination when it allows all these other schools to discriminate. Colorado doesn't get to pick and choose who gets to discriminate and call its law generally applicable.
- Colorado allows UPK schools designed to service disabled children to deny enrollment to children without disabilities. This is neccessary to ensure that disabled children have enough space at those schools. If the special needs school enrolls children that could go anywhere then that's fewer seats available for disabled children. Head Start schools similarly provide special instruction for low income children and families. If Head Start schools enroll children that could go anywhere then that's fewer seats available for low income children who require this speicalized instruction. Discrimination in these cases are required to persue Colorado's interest in equal opportunity to enroll and recieve preschool.
- Odean was wrong when she testified that schools could admit only gender-nonconforming children, children of color, or lgbtq children. There is no discretion in accepting the equal opportunity requirements. The UKP does not have the power to waive the requirements. The equal opportunity requirements apply to everyone.
** Does Carson v Makin displace Employment Division v Smith only when the government explicitly excludes religious institutions?
- In Carson Maine's tuition assistance program was unavailble to religious schools. Here the UPK system is a tuition assistance program that is unavailable to petitioner's religious schools. Carson displaces Employment Division and triggers strict scrutiny.
- In Carson Maine's tuition assistance explicitly excluded religious education. Employment Division involved neutral, generally applicable laws, which only incidently burdened religious exercise. Employment Division holds triggering rational basis review.
